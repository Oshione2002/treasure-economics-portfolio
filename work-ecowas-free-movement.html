ECOWAS free movement, trade and Nigeria’s economic growth

Abstract

This study examines how regional migration and trade relate to Nigeria’s economic growth over 1981–2024. Drawing on an endogenous-growth framework, the analysis uses an autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) and error-correction approach. Real GDP growth is modelled against ECOWAS shares of Nigeria’s net migration and world trade, alongside investment, labour-force participation, social and community-services expenditure and inflation.

The reported estimates associate migration and regional trade with positive current-period growth relationships, followed by weaker or negative lagged relationships. This pattern suggests that the benefits of regional integration may depend on domestic capacity to absorb labour, expand production and support cross-border commerce. The study emphasises migrant documentation, skills matching, border infrastructure, export capacity and predictable rules. Migration and trade shares are imperfect measures of the free-movement protocol itself, while national annual data conceal differences across sectors and border regions. Data gaps and structural changes also constrain interpretation; the estimates do not establish that the protocol caused the observed growth outcomes.
