02 / Research collaboration / Regional integration

ECOWAS free movement, trade and Nigeria’s economic growth

A dynamic study of whether regional mobility and trade created durable growth gains for Nigeria—or short-run benefits followed by adjustment pressure.

Research question

Can regional mobility widen markets without overwhelming domestic capacity?

The ECOWAS protocol aims to support entry, residence and economic activity across member states. For Nigeria, the potential gains from labour mobility and cross-border commerce sit beside concerns about informality, weak border governance and pressure on institutions.

Framework and design

Growth through labour, trade and knowledge—with domestic conditions in the model.

The study used a Romer-type endogenous-growth framework. Real GDP growth was modelled against ECOWAS migration and trade shares, gross fixed capital formation, labour-force participation, social and community-services expenditure and inflation.

Mobility proxyECOWAS share of Nigeria’s net migration
Trade proxyECOWAS share of Nigeria’s world trade
Domestic controlsInvestment, labour and social spending
Stability controlInflation

Reported evidence

The direction changed over time.

Migration / current periodPositive association
Migration / first lagNegative association
Regional trade / current periodPositive association
Regional trade / later lagsNegative associations

The model reports an initial growth benefit from greater regional migration and trade shares, followed by weaker or negative lagged effects. Labour and social spending showed a similar pattern, while lagged inflation generally weakened growth.

Interpretation

Integration gains were conditional, not automatic.

Mobility can expand labour supply, entrepreneurship and knowledge flows. Regional trade can widen markets. But those gains are harder to sustain when job creation, infrastructure, formalisation and security coordination do not keep pace. The policy emphasis is therefore on better migrant documentation, skills matching, border infrastructure, export capacity and predictable rules—not simply more or less movement.

Limitations

What remains uncertain.

  • Migration and trade shares are imperfect proxies for the effect of the protocol itself.
  • Annual national data cannot show how effects differ by sector, border region or migrant group.
  • The long period contains structural shifts, border closures and security shocks.
  • Migration data are less complete than standard macroeconomic series.
  • The work has not been peer reviewed or formally published.
