Monetary policy rate and bank loans to small enterprises

Abstract

This study examines the relationship between Nigeria’s monetary policy rate and commercial-bank loans to small-scale enterprises using 32 annual observations for 1992–2023. Data from the Central Bank of Nigeria and World Bank indicators were analysed through descriptive statistics, unit-root tests, autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) bounds testing, long-run estimation and an error-correction representation. The model relates logged loans to the monetary policy rate, prime lending rate, private-sector credit and inflation.

The reported long-run monetary-policy-rate coefficient is positive, contrary to the conventional expectation that tighter policy reduces lending. This is an association within the estimated model, not evidence that raising policy rates causes additional SME credit. Interpretation requires particular caution: the manuscript’s original abstract described a negative relationship, whereas its coefficient table and detailed discussion reported a positive one. A positive error-correction term also prevents an unqualified claim of stable adjustment. The small sample, omitted reforms and other credit-market influences limit conclusions. The study points to the need for further specification and robustness checks before drawing firm policy recommendations.
