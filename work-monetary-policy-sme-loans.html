Monetary policy rate and bank loans to small enterprises

A compact annual time-series model examining how Nigeria’s monetary policy rate, lending conditions, credit and inflation relate to loans for small-scale enterprises.

Research question

Does tighter monetary policy reduce formal credit to small firms?

The study modelled the natural log of commercial-bank loans to small-scale enterprises against the monetary policy rate, prime lending rate, private-sector credit and inflation.

Design

A mixed-integration time-series workflow.

Descriptive and correlation analysis was followed by ADF and PP unit-root tests. Because the variables were a mixture of I(0) and I(1), the analysis used ARDL bounds testing, long-run estimation, an error-correction representation and residual diagnostics.

Model output

The reported coefficient was positive—not negative.

The estimated ARDL specification associated a one-point rise in MPR with an increase in logged SME lending, holding the included controls constant. That direction is counterintuitive and should be interpreted as an association, potentially shaped by credit directives, omitted reforms or common trends.

Quality-control note

The manuscript contained an internal contradiction.

Editorial correction

The abstract described a negative long-run effect, while the coefficient table, detailed interpretation and conclusion reported a positive effect. This portfolio follows the model table and flags the inconsistency rather than repeating the abstract’s claim.

The error-correction term was also positive, which is atypical for a conventional convergence mechanism. Its significance alone is not enough to claim stable adjustment toward equilibrium.

Limitations

Why the result needs caution.

  • Only 32 annual observations were available.
  • The period includes financial reforms and macroeconomic regime changes that were not modelled as structural breaks.
  • Bank health, output growth and directed-credit policies were omitted.
  • Aggregate bank-loan data do not capture informal or non-bank SME finance.
  • The paper has not been peer reviewed or formally published.
