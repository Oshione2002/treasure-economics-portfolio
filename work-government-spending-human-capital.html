Government spending and human capital development in Nigeria

Abstract

This study examines the relationship between government expenditure on education and health and human capital development in Nigeria over 1990–2022. Annual secondary data were analysed using an autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) model and an error-correction framework, supported by unit-root and diagnostic tests. Total, recurrent and capital expenditure were considered alongside five outcomes: primary school enrolment, literacy, life expectancy, infant mortality and maternal mortality.

The reported results indicate positive relationships between education expenditure and enrolment and literacy, and between health expenditure and life expectancy. Health expenditure was also associated with lower infant and maternal mortality. The findings suggest that sustained investment in education and health should be accompanied by more effective allocation, transparency and service delivery. The relatively small annual sample and aggregate expenditure measures limit what can be inferred about spending efficiency and differences across regions. These time-series relationships should be interpreted as associations rather than definitive causal effects.
