Government spending and human capital development in Nigeria

Abstract

Nigeria has consistently faced challenges in human capital development, particularly in the areas of education and healthcare. Despite various government interventions, low school enrollment, poor literacy rates, high infant and maternal mortality, and low life expectancy remain persistent issues. A major concern is whether government expenditure in these sectors has been effective in improving human capital outcomes. Previous studies have focused primarily on economic growth, neglecting the direct impact of government spending on human development indicators. This study seeks to fill this gap by examining the relationship between government expenditure on education and health and human capital development in Nigeria from 1990 to 2022.

Using secondary data sourced from the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), and the World Bank, this study employs the Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model and the Error Correction Model (ECM) to analyze both long-run and short-run relationships. The findings reveal that government spending on education significantly influences primary school enrollment and literacy rates, while expenditure on health positively impacts life expectancy and reduces infant and maternal mortality rates. However, inefficiencies in fund allocation, corruption, and inconsistent policy implementation have limited the effectiveness of these investments. The study establishes a strong long-run relationship between public spending and human capital development, underscoring the importance of sustained and well-targeted government investment.

Based on these findings, the study recommends increased government spending to meet international benchmarks, improved transparency and accountability in public finance, equitable allocation of resources, stronger public-private partnerships, and enhanced healthcare service delivery. By addressing these challenges, Nigeria can make significant progress in improving human capital development, which is crucial for long-term economic growth and national development.

Keywords: Government Expenditure, Human Capital Development, Education, Health, Nigeria, ARDL, ECM.
