Domestic debt, external debt and economic growth in Nigeria

Abstract

This study examines whether domestic and external public debt have different relationships with economic growth in Nigeria over 1981–2023. Annual data were analysed using autoregressive distributed lag (ARDL) and error-correction models, supported by stationarity and diagnostic tests. Inflation, interest rates, exchange rates and foreign direct investment were included as controls, while Granger tests examined predictive relationships between borrowing and growth.

The reported findings associate domestic debt with short-run contraction that weakens over time, while external debt shows conditional short-run support that can be undermined by exchange-rate and servicing pressures. The Granger tests indicate bidirectional predictive relationships between debt and growth, rather than definitive structural causation. The study concludes that productive use, financing costs, currency exposure and fiscal discipline matter alongside the source of borrowing. Aggregate debt measures cannot identify the productivity of individual debt-financed projects, and the modelling approach may miss nonlinear thresholds and structural changes. The results therefore support a context-sensitive assessment of debt composition rather than a simple preference for domestic or external borrowing.
